Our team selected **JACpot**, a campus-specific marketplace for John Abbott College students. - **Buying and selling:** students post listings, browse items, search and filter results, and chat about an item.
- **Requests:** a message board lets students ask for something they cannot find in current listings.

- **Trust and safety:** full names and student IDs were raised as possible identity signals, along with ratings, in-app warnings, and suggested public meetup spots such as Hertzberg Library, the Agora, and the Zen Zone.
- **Payment:** JACpot would not process purchases. Students would arrange payment in person.

## Targeted research findings

### Target users, needs, and pain points

Our target users are **John Abbott College students** who want to buy, sell, or request useful items from other students, particularly course materials. From our discussion, we identified three likely problems:

1. Relevant posts are scattered across Facebook groups and Facebook Marketplace.
2. General listings may omit textbook details students care about, such as course information or whether an access code has already been used.
3. It can be difficult to tell whether the person on the other side of a transaction belongs to the college community.

These are working findings from our team's experience and discussion, **not survey results**. Student interviews or a short survey would help test which problems matter most.

### Existing options and gaps

| Current option              | What it offers                                    | Gap JACpot could address                                                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| John Abbott Facebook groups | Familiar spaces for student posts and replies.    | Posts can be spread across groups and hard to filter by item type or textbook details.                                             |
| Facebook Marketplace        | Listings, search, and messaging at a large scale. | The audience is broader than JAC, and campus verification, textbook-specific fields, and campus meetup guidance are not its focus. |

This comparison reflects the options our team discussed. Before implementation, we should check their current features and ask students how they actually use them.

### Domain and technical constraints

- **Verification:** We would need to decide how JAC email verification works and what happens when a student graduates or loses access to their college email.
- **Privacy:** Full names and student IDs could help identify users, but student IDs are sensitive. We should verify eligibility privately and display only the information needed for an exchange.
- **Safety:** Ratings and warnings can help students make decisions, but they cannot guarantee safety. Reporting and blocking tools, plus suggestions for public meetup locations, would be important.
- **Scope:** Keeping payment in person avoids building payment processing, refunds, and delivery into the first version.
- **Content quality:** Listings and requests would need categories, moderation, and a way to mark items sold or remove stale posts. Textbook listings should include useful fields such as title, course, and access-code status.

## Team decision and final selection

We selected JACpot by **consensus**. Since the team was already interested in a campus marketplace, our choice was mainly about shaping it into a viable project rather than voting among unrelated app ideas. We kept the idea because it serves a clear John Abbott audience, addresses a recognizable problem, and can be built in stages.

Our proposed first version centers on listings, search and filters, requests, profiles, verification, and chat. Ratings and safety guidance would support trust, while in-person payment keeps the scope manageable. The used access-code tag is one small, concrete way a student-focused marketplace could be more useful than a general one.

**Decision record:** Final domain — John Abbott College student marketplace. Method — team consensus. Main reasons — campus relevance, one place to find student listings and requests, useful textbook details, and a feasible first version. We did not record a formal vote or ranked alternatives, so none are claimed here.

## Final project vision

**Target user/client:** John Abbott College students buying, selling, or requesting items from other students.

**Core problem:** Student exchanges are scattered across general platforms, where relevant posts are hard to find and student-specific details are easy to miss.

**App category:** A campus-specific, peer-to-peer marketplace.

**Tech-driven unique solution:** JACpot would combine JAC email-verified accounts with searchable listings, requests, in-app chat, and textbook-specific fields such as used access-code status. Profiles, ratings, warnings, and suggested public meetup spots would help students make informed choices for in-person exchanges. The app would not handle payment.

## 60-second elevator pitch

Need a textbook, a used item for school, or someone on campus who has what you are looking for? Right now, John Abbott students often search through different Facebook groups and Marketplace posts, then have to figure out whether a listing is relevant or the seller is actually a student. JACpot brings those exchanges into one campus marketplace. Students can post listings or requests, search and filter items, and chat through JAC email-verified accounts. Textbook listings would show details that matter, including whether a used access code is included. Profiles, ratings, safety warnings, and suggested public meetup spots help students make informed choices. Payments would happen in person, so the app stays focused on connecting students. JACpot is a simpler way for our campus community to find what it needs from one another.
